## Zone Classification in Explosive Environments

In explosion protection engineering, hazardous areas are divided into two main types:

- **Gas environments** (symbol: **G**)
- **Dust environments** (symbol: **D**)

Based on the **presence and duration** of flammable gases or dust, environments are categorized into **Zones**.

---

## Zone Classification Table

| Gas Symbol (G)           | Dust Symbol (D)           | Environmental Characteristics                                                                               |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Zone 0** (Category 1G) | **Zone 20** (Category 1D) | An explosive atmosphere is present **continuously, for long periods, or frequently** (&gt;1000 hours/year). |
| **Zone 1** (Category 2G) | **Zone 21** (Category 2D) | An explosive atmosphere is **likely to occur under normal operation** (10–1000 hours/year).                 |
| **Zone 2** (Category 3G) | **Zone 22** (Category 3D) | An explosive atmosphere is **unlikely**, and if it occurs, it is **short-term only** (&lt;10 hours/year).   |

---

## Important Notes

- The **smaller the zone number**, the **higher the explosion risk**.
- A device certified for a **more hazardous zone** (e.g., Zone 0) can also be used in **less hazardous zones** (e.g., Zone 1 or 2).

---

## What if both gas (G) and dust (D) are present?

→ Choose the **more hazardous** of the two to determine whether the area should be treated as a **G** or **D** zone.

This classification helps determine:

- The **ignition source**
- The correct **equipment group** and **types of protection** needed
