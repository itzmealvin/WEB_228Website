## 1. Classification of electrical/electronic equipment (Equipment group) based on usage area

There are 2 usage environments:

| Code | Usage Area                                                                                         |
| ---- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| I    | Mining areas such as coal mines, onshore and offshore oil fields – very high risk of explosion.    |
| II   | Other industrial areas such as gas stations, factories, construction sites – lower explosion risk. |

---

## 2. Types of protection

### 📌 Codes in gas environments (G)

- **Ex d**: flameproof enclosure that prevents ignition from escaping.
- **Ex p**: positive pressure inside the enclosure to prevent flammable gas ingress (px, py, pz based on zone).
- **Ex q**: filled enclosure using quartz or glass powder.
- **Ex o**: equipment immersed in oil.
- **Ex e**: enhanced safety design to reduce spark generation.
- **Ex i**: intrinsic safety by controlling electrical energy (ia, ib, ic).
- **Ex n**: non-sparking design – typically requires IP65 or higher (nA, nR, nC, nL, nZ).
- **Ex m**: encapsulation to isolate spark-producing components (ma, mb).

### 📌 Codes in dust environments (D)

- **tD Xxx IPxx Txx**
- **pD xx IPxx Txx**
- **ixD xx Txx**
- **mD xx Txx**

---

## 3. Explosion groups

- **Group I**: used in mining environments (e.g., coal dust, methane gas).
- **Group II**: used in surface industries, further divided into:
  - **IIA**: Propane
  - **IIB**: Ethylene
  - **IIC**: Hydrogen, Acetylene

> ⚠️ Equipment certified for **IIC** can be used in **IIA** and **IIB** environments, but not the other way around.
